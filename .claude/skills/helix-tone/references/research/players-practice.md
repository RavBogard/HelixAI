### Amp dial-in method

  with any amp ("my 101"). He credits Tim Pierce for the rule that slightly crunchy still
  reads as clean in a mix. (FULL Gig Preset Walkthrough, 5:19-6:50)
- He does not like compressor cleans; he wants the amp to break up a little if he slams the
  bridge pickup, and to clean up on the middle position. (From Scratch LIVE, 29:35-30:17)
- JTM 45: mids dimed up, less broken up, treble and presence "always winding up coming
  down" because there is a high cut in the amp block and in the cab block. (From Scratch

### Leveling presets and snapshots (the headline method)

- Method: record the Helix into a DAW (he uses Cakewalk) with a bare bypassed signal; the
  meter reads "about minus 12 dB or a little bit hotter". Match every preset to roughly -12
  on that meter. Output left untouched, big knob not in play. (Level Presets, 2:48-3:56)
- Which level: channel volume on the amp, not drive, master or output. (Level Presets,
  9:31-9:45)
- He is explicit this is not exact, "gets me in the ballpark... doesn't get me on the
  field yet"; the final trim is done with the band in rehearsal. (Level Presets, 3:56-4:11,
  4:49-5:11)
- He leaves a preset at -12 even when it sounds wrong at home (cleans seem weak, dirty seem
  buried) and tweaks in rehearsal. Reason: perceived volume is unreliable, so he does not
  monitor while matching. (Level Presets, 4:18-4:57)
- Preset that measured -18 dB: he raised channel volume until it hit the target, and said
  too much then "compresses" from being driven, so he backed it off. At -12 "it comes to
  life", which he calls his experience, not a technical fact. (Level Presets, 9:26-10:28)
- No lead boost in dB is given in the walkthrough; his solo boost is a "little" gain
  and volume bump (OVERTHINKING 9:49-10:03; "Horizon Drive level hot", FULL Gig Preset
  Walkthrough 18:23). On the snapshots: the crunch EQ boost is 2 dB (From Scratch LIVE
  37:51).
- Philosophy: keep the preset at a normal volume; any pro soundboard can add gain; raising
  amp gain without lowering channel volume is "the biggest mistake". If a gained-up preset
  is louder, the clean preset then has no headroom, and you get a distorted clean amp. Add
  gain, then bring channel volume down. Cleans and gained presets should sit at similar
  volume. In his case no engineer has ever asked for more gain. (OVERTHINKING, 11:51-15:16)
- Hot channel/master/gain "all cranked" gives fizzy, crackling distortion, "the number
  one problem" in lessons he has given. (Level Presets, 1:34-2:01)

### Snapshots vs stomp mode vs Command Center

- Why stomp-only: with Command Center a single footswitch can step through four
  snapshots (the HX snapshot command with a target of 2, then 3, then 4, then 1; each
  press resets the command to "next" and you must re-set the destination), while every
  other footswitch keeps normal bypass duty. (Use THIS instead of Snapshot Mode, 1:59-6:03)
- He calls it a trap: you must set the assignment on each snapshot, because the command
  resets after each press. (Use THIS instead of Snapshot Mode, 3:10-4:46)
- A footswitch can also jump to any preset number, and parameter controls can sit on a
  footswitch with min and max values. Example: Air-Apparent-type gain controlled between
  2.4 (min) and 4 (max) by footswitch 2 while on. (Use THIS instead of Snapshot Mode,
  6:16-8:12)
- "About 32 assignments" are available per preset. (Use THIS instead of Snapshot Mode,
  8:18-8:21; "I think there's like 32")
- Solo button: one footswitch (6) controls mix, channel volume, drive and cut, in any
  snapshot. (Use THIS instead of Snapshot Mode, 8:21-9:00)
- HX Stomp (three snapshots) trick for a known setlist: copy a song's preset into two or
  three slots lettered A, B, C, each with its own set of snapshots; press the two
  footswitches at once while still playing to open a blinking menu to jump to the next
  preset, which lands on the snapshot you saved there. He cycles through six snapshots
  this way. There is a short audible hiccup and he says nobody notices if timed on a rest.
  (HX Stomp Snapshot Trick, 1:46-2:00, 4:11-5:03, 7:23-8:05)
- On the big Helix he used snapshots for each song section on a touring song ("enough
  footswitches to get away with all this"). (HX Stomp Snapshot Trick, 1:30-1:41)

### Building a gig preset from scratch

- One "master preset" per gig that covers all bases, at most two or three presets
  (extra preset only for something like pitch effects). Reasons: changes cannot be
  maintained across many presets; sound changes from song to song; things drift on a gig and
  you need one thing to fix; people rarely notice the tone. "I've never had anyone say
  that's not exactly the right tone for Taylor Swift." "Your drummer doesn't change their kit
  between songs." (Gigging a Modeler, 2:05-5:41, 8:46-8:57, 10:14-10:28)
- Keep it simple: few snapshots, short chain, so a change is "a right I just quickly do this"
  job, not a ten-minute menu dive. (Gigging a Modeler, 5:44-6:57, 14:06-14:30)
- Example preset: drive pedal up front, phaser, clean amp, dirty amp, cab, delay; snapshots
  for clean, solo, rhythm. (Gigging a Modeler, 10:32-11:20, 14:09-14:22)

- His own amp notes (a real tube amp via load box, model unclear in the caption):

  mids halfway, treble about 7.5, presence "a lot higher than I'd normally have", and says
  he would normally have presence and treble low and mids and bass pushed. (Dialling in Your
  AMP WRONG, 7:58-9:12, 12:44-16:21)

### Leveling presets and snapshots

- No meter, no target number. Method by ear and playing: play the clean as loud as you
  would actually play it (for funky stuff, a hard snappy attack), then set the rhythm tone
  "on par with the loudest part of your clean playing, or slightly louder", then the solo
  louder again: "maybe a dB, a couple of dB". Reason: live you play louder than you did when
  matching, a snappy clean does not compress and a distorted tone does, so a driven tone at
  equal "perceived" volume ends up quieter than the clean. (Gigging a Modeler, 7:00-8:46)

## Worship Tutorials (Helix video: the narrator is unnamed; the Quad Cortex video is Bradford)

The Helix video says "Bradford and I" build patches, the speaker is the other
presenter, unnamed in the transcript. He builds on a Helix floor, into a Universal Audio

(Helix Patch from Scratch, 0:49-1:40) The patch rebuilt is their free AC30 patch.

### How they structure a patch (Helix video, in the order he builds)

1. Global settings first: global pad off for most guitars (on only for hot or active
   pickups); input left on Auto or "multi"; outputs left on "multi". (Helix Patch from Scratch,
   2:21-3:30)
2. Use both paths: set path 2 input to "2 to A" (connect the two paths), because each path
   is its own DSP chip and filling only path 1 means you run out of DSP early. (Helix Patch
   from Scratch, 3:00-3:50)
3. Volume pedal first in the chain, mono; clear its auto-assigned toe-switch; assign
   volume to EXP 2; curve logarithmic ("a more subtle ramp", better for swells). (Helix
   Patch from Scratch, 3:50-4:52)
4. Core: amp, cab, compressor, and an EQ at the end of the chain. (Helix Patch from
   Scratch, 4:56-5:07)
5. Wet effects (delays and reverbs) after the amp: "always sounds better... keeps the wet
   effects cleaner"; he admits he cannot say why and that great players put them before.
   (Helix Patch from Scratch, 21:58-22:35)
6. Make everything stereo from the first stereo block onward; a mono EQ will sum the chain,
   and converting the EQ to stereo resets its settings. (Helix Patch from Scratch, 22:37-23:30)
7. Delays: quarter-note tape (transistor tape), dotted-eighth (vintage digital), dual delay
   (any stereo delay turned into a dual delay with scale and spread). (Helix Patch from
   Scratch, 23:56-32:20)
8. Reverbs: a subtle hall, then a big ambient (Glitz). (Helix Patch from Scratch, 32:30-37:24)
9. Drives: three cascaded drives, Heir Apparent first, then tube-screamer-style, then a
   Dyna-type drive as the "bigger distortion-ish"; Timmy also mentioned as another
   low-gain option. (Helix Patch from Scratch, 37:31-44:41)
10. Swells: a second Glitz reverb driven by a multi-tap delay, both on one footswitch.
    (Helix Patch from Scratch, 44:47-49:18)
11. Modulation: tremolo, then 70s chorus (Boss CE-2 type). (Helix Patch from Scratch, 49:23-51:20)
12. Snapshots last: "clean" (dotted-eighth delay and hall verb), paste snapshot 1 into snapshot
    2 and turn on a drive, rename, build out the rest. (Helix Patch from Scratch, 54:04-54:46)
13. Boost: a Gain block at the end of the chain, "say three dB of gain", on a footswitch
    (11) for a pure solo volume bump. He says it often sounds cleaner and more present
    than adding another overdrive. (Helix Patch from Scratch, 55:15-57:24)

### Amp dial-in method

- Set the amp so it just starts to break up: on bridge pickup strum hard and the amp
  breaks up a little; the middle position stays clean. Gain "around five, five and a half",
  later about 6. He says the gain level "will affect how everything sounds when you start
  pushing it with overdrive". (Helix Patch from Scratch, 8:16-10:24)
- Master volume changes the tone: he runs it around "eight, eight and a half" ("seven and a
  half, eight-ish" later), uses channel volume to set overall level. Marshalls "are notorious" for
  going bassy when the master is all the way up. (Helix Patch from Scratch, 7:15-8:13)
- Cut on the AC30 model rolls off highs; treble up and cut up can sound better than treble
  down. (Helix Patch from Scratch, 9:40-10:12)
- Presence as "a second treble"; sag and bias set by feel. (Helix Patch from Scratch, 10:25-11:50)
- Mid frequency EQ is "the magic": mid centred between 1.6 and 2.4 (units not said, kHz by
  context), boost "like 3 dB", then trim the EQ level by 2 dB so the on/off level is the
  same. "That's going to really help you sit well in a mix." (Helix Patch from Scratch,
  16:23-17:55)

### Cab and mic choices and cuts

- Always dual cab. For an AC30: Blue Bell with a 160 ribbon and Silver Bell with a 57 ("I'm
  a fan of pairing a dynamic mic with a ribbon"); mics up close; high cut about 6.5k and
  low cut about 80 ("ish") then "use my ears". (Helix Patch from Scratch, 5:36-6:33)

  high cut to "like five K". (Helix Patch from Scratch, 20:24-21:17)

### Compression (Helix video)

### Delay and reverb numbers (a worship patch's core)

- Tape delay quarter, trails on, tempo 90 BPM; feedback "about one measure", initial plus
  three repeats. Scale 100 and spread 10 for widest ping-pong. Mix about 25%. (Helix Patch
  from Scratch, 24:07-25:40)
- Vintage digital dotted eighth: mix about 34-35 ("around there"), more feedback, bit
  depth 24 for highest fidelity, 16 for pleasant degrade, below that "nasty"; sample rate
  48 k is highest, 8 kHz is "really dark, really warm". He likes about 24 or 16 bit.
  (Helix Patch from Scratch, 25:58-29:01)
- Dual delay by changing a stereo delay's scale: quarter with scale 75% gives quarter plus
  dotted eighth ping-ponging. Dotted-eighth with scale 67% gives eighth plus dotted-eighth
  (the correct value is 66.67%). (Helix Patch from Scratch, 29:48-32:20)
- Hall reverb: time about 5, trails on, pre-delay a little, high cut pulled down to
  darken, mix long and low. Glitz: time 6 to 6.5, a little pre-delay, mix 20 to 22, trails on,
  mod mix lowered because it sat better in the mix. (Helix Patch from Scratch, 32:30-37:10)
- Swell multi-tap: initial time about 1.2, not tempo-locked ("slow song will wash forever"),
  feedback high, diffusion high, mix 40 to 45, tap scales 100, 67, 33, 25 percent, all taps
  level 100; the second Glitz with longer decay and mix up by "ten percent more" (caption
  "thirty four") unclear. (Helix Patch from Scratch, 44:47-49:00)
