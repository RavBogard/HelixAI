## 1. His build order

Two versions: the short "4 Steps" and the longer 2025 walk-through. The 2026
Stadium video follows the 2025 one.

**4 Steps (the short rule)**
1. Choose the amp for the end goal; "a very personal choice" (4 Steps, 0:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y7wLssljDk).
2. Choose the cab, "probably the most important step"; do this before touching the amp controls (4 Steps, 1:03, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y7wLssljDk).
3. Choose the mic and its placement, "pretty much equally as important as choosing the right cab" (4 Steps, 2:57, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y7wLssljDk).
4. Go back to the amp and set the tone stack (treble, presence, bass, mids) now that cab and mic give a good base (4 Steps, 4:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y7wLssljDk).
5. Add the low/high shelf EQ at 650 Hz, which he calls step four (4 Steps, 6:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y7wLssljDk). He says he "revisits" the cab distance again after the tone stack, nudging the mic a little closer (4 Steps, 5:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y7wLssljDk).

**2025 (the fuller sequence)**
1. Set Path A's output to "Path 2 A" so the signal runs on into path B and both DSP engines can be used (2025, 1:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg). Stadium video does the same (2026 Stadium, 0:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20).
2. Input block: gate on, impedance Auto, guitar pad off unless the guitar is very high output (2025, 2:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg). No threshold or decay numbers given; he says they depend on the guitar and gain and must be set while playing (2025, 2:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
3. Add the amp block, not as the very first block, so he can add things before it later (2025, 3:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg). Warns newcomers who forget the cab and hear a buzzy tone (2025, 4:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg). Choosing "cab" from the block list replaces a selected amp, so pick an empty slot first (2025, 4:49, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
4. Choose the cab. "Never underestimate how much of an effect... changing the cab is going to have" (2025, 5:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
5. Choose the mic, then its position and angle (2025, 6:02-8:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg). He says it "doesn't have to be perfect at this point" (2025, 8:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
6. Set gain level with snapshots: a different Drive per snapshot with channel volume moved the opposite way to keep the level matched (2025, 9:37, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg). Values shown: roughly 2.1 on the clean snapshot up to about 7.2 on the heavy one; the 2.1 figure is garbled in the caption (2025, 10:41-10:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
7. Tone stack: here he takes bass down a little and adds presence/treble (2025, 11:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
8. Reverb on path 2 (stereo if the preset is stereo), trails on (2025, 12:19-12:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
9. Shelf EQ right after the amp/cab (2025, 14:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
10. Compressor at the very end, "even after my time effects", stereo if anything before it is stereo (2025, 17:07-17:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
11. Delay (transistor tape) "just in case I need it" (2025, 19:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
12. Parametric EQ used only as low/high cut, placed right before the compressor and kept stereo (2025, 21:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
13. Last: final output block to set preset level against other presets (2025, 22:57, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
14. Then a snapshot with every "sweetener" (cuts, comp, shelf, reverb, delay) bypassed to A/B against the bare tone (2025, 23:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg). Same idea in Stadium: snapshot 1 sweeteners off, snapshot 2 on (2026 Stadium, 15:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20).

Core of his method in one line: the amp and cab decisions "shape pretty much 90% of our tone", and the chain after them is a "little mastering section" of cuts, shelf, compressor, small reverb and delay (2026 Stadium, 2:19 and 8:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20; "mastering section", Pt. 6 EQ, 52:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROT-mWdFlOU).

**Counter-position from the same man.** In Pt. 35 he shows that an amp plus its stock paired cab at factory settings, with no cuts, no compressor and no IR, is already usable, and says this "goes against a lot of my other videos" (Pt. 35 Simple, 25:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F6MEgl4pBlk). His advice there is to adapt your playing (roll the volume back, play softer or finger-pick) rather than over-process (Pt. 35 Simple, 10:11 and 23:01, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F6MEgl4pBlk). Even then, his own quick tweak on the Brit Plexi Bright was a ribbon mic pulled back, LA Studio Comp, a touch of room reverb and transistor tape (Pt. 35 Simple, 23:26-24:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F6MEgl4pBlk).

## 2. Amp controls

### Drive, Master, Channel Volume

- For the Sag/Hum/Bias demos he sets Master (and sometimes Drive) to 10, because the manual says those power-section controls react much more with Master at 10. He says plainly these are not settings he would normally use (Pt. 4 Sag, 3:19-3:49, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).
- He matches level between snapshots by moving Channel Volume against Drive and Sag. With Sag at 10 the level drops, so he raised Channel Volume to compensate (Pt. 4 Sag, 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak). In 2025, raising Drive made the volume "jump a lot" and he pulled Channel Volume down (2025, 10:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
- A more realistic setting from the vintage-voiced test: Master back to 6.5 and Drive to about 7, "a whole different tone altogether... softens it up a bit" (Pt. 4 Sag, 25:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).
- On Marshall-style amps (Brit Plexi): he likes cranking Bass, Middle, Treble all to 10, "the tone stack on these Marshalls is not as dramatic", and may take Presence all the way up ("might be a little much") (2026 Stadium, 7:15-7:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20). On the Brit Plexi Bright he opened everything up (bass, middle, treble, presence) and pulled Channel Volume back (Pt. 35 Simple, 15:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F6MEgl4pBlk). He also says Plexi "sounds great with the mids cranked up to 10" (Pt. 6 EQ, 4:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROT-mWdFlOU).
- He says amp tone-stack knobs have no defined frequency, vary by amp, and he sets them by ear (Pt. 6 EQ, 3:29-4:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROT-mWdFlOU).
- Stadium only: amp Level defaults to -10; he raised it to 0 and saved it as the user default for that amp (2026 Stadium, 3:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20). He leaves the Stadium "Hype" knob at zero (2026 Stadium, 7:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20). Stadium adds a cab automatically when you add the amp (2026 Stadium, 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20).

### Sag

- What he says it is: voltage drop on a transient, then recovery; acts like a compressor, squashes the pick attack, "squishy" (Pt. 4 Sag, 5:45-6:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).
- Not wanted for modern metal or crisp attack, "like a compressor with a really fast attack time" squashing the transient (Pt. 4 Sag, 6:31-6:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).
- Default is about 5 and he says most amp defaults sit near the middle (Pt. 4 Sag, 4:57, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).
- Sag 0: flat, "not a dynamic sound" (Pt. 4 Sag, 7:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak). Sag 10: attack "just squash[ed] down" and overall level lower (Pt. 4 Sag, 8:09-8:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).
- Practical advice: do not go to 10; try 7 or 8 for more squish; a player who cannot set a compressor well might use Sag instead (Pt. 4 Sag, 9:58-10:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak). Also: "tweak up the sag by a point or two" may be the missing sound (Pt. 4 Sag, 30:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).
- He says you feel it more than hear it when playing (Pt. 4 Sag, 9:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).
- He turned dynamics off in the test patches so a compressor would not hide what Sag and Bias do to feel (Pt. 4 Sag, 4:07-4:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).

### Hum

- Hum 10 gives an audible low "almost like 60 cycle hum"; also makes note tails break up "warbly" and "rude" (Pt. 4 Sag, 10:51 and 11:42-12:57, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak). Hum 0 removes it (Pt. 4 Sag, 12:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).
- His use: a little for an authentic old-recording sound, or to imitate a tone you are copying (Pt. 4 Sag, 11:22-11:32 and 12:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).
- He gives no specific Hum value he uses in real presets; default is 5 (Pt. 4 Sag, 10:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).

### Ripple

- He is least sure about it. His belief: residual AC left after rectification ends up in the signal (Pt. 4 Sag, 13:47-14:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).
- Effect: "a slight little bit more of a rude nastiness", the least dramatic of the set; seemed to do more with Hum at 10, which he flags as only an observation (Pt. 4 Sag, 13:56 and 15:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).

### Bias

- His understanding (he says he has never biased a tube): idle current through the tubes; too hot shortens tube life; hot bias is "usually preferred" sonically, cold gives "uglier" distortion with fewer wanted harmonics (Pt. 4 Sag, 15:53-16:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).
- Default is 5 (the Brit amp he used shows 5.5) (Pt. 4 Sag, 5:03 and 17:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).
- Bias 0: "very different distortion, very edgy, almost abrasive". Bias 5: less distortion but "more pleasant musical" (Pt. 4 Sag, 17:54-18:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak). The caption does not describe what Bias 10 sounded like (jump at 17:26).
- Practical use: instead of fixing a buzzy or fizzy edge with mic and cab choice, try Bias 6, 6.5 or 7 to take the edge off the distortion (Pt. 4 Sag, 18:56-19:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).

### Bias X

- His description: "more to do with the voicing of the power tubes"; "set low it's going to have a tighter feel and set high it's gonna be more dynamic", a voicing change rather than compression (Pt. 4 Sag, 19:25-19:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).
- In the A/B he says one extreme gives a softer attack and warmer sound overall, but the caption does not make clear whether that was 0 or 10 (Pt. 4 Sag, 20:07-20:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak). Unclear; do not rely on direction from the second description.
- "How powerful... that control is" (Pt. 4 Sag, 21:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).

### Combined settings test

- "Vintage squishy" snapshot: higher Hum, Ripple, Bias, Bias X, "classic squishy dynamic sound"; "tight" snapshot: no Hum, Bias a bit cold or Bias X low, less Sag, "less dynamic sound" (Pt. 4 Sag, 22:30-23:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak). No numbers given for either except "everything at 5" for the default snapshot.
- He says the cold/low snapshot was very edgy and "barely cleans up at all" (Pt. 4 Sag, 23:40-23:57, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak). The caption is ambiguous about which of the two snapshots this describes; the sentence follows the "tight" one.

### Early reflections (cab block control, not amp)

- He describes it as like a reverb pre-delay: first reflections coming back to the mic, to make the cab feel "in a room" (Pt. 4 Sag, 26:35-27:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).
- 100% is "almost a slap back"; 50% subtler; his own use "around 30 percent, 25, 30, 35, 40" and says it is "fairly subtle" (Pt. 4 Sag, 27:54-28:50 and 29:45-30:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak). Tested with the reverb off so it can be heard; with reverb on he suggests 50 ms of reverb pre-delay (Pt. 4 Sag, 27:29 and 29:08, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).

### His testing method

- If unsure what a control does, set it to both extremes and listen, then back off (Pt. 4 Sag, 2:13-2:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak; same in Pt. 6 EQ, 47:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROT-mWdFlOU). Copy the patch to another slot and use snapshots to A/B (Pt. 4 Sag, 30:55-31:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).

### Mic choice

- He is a "huge fan of ribbon mics" and goes to the 121 or 160 ribbon, sometimes 4038 or 84 ribbon, "to almost every single preset" for a warmer tone and a good mix starting point (4 Steps, 3:06-3:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y7wLssljDk; 2026 Stadium, 4:28-4:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20).
- Character he gives: 121 "a little fuller and maybe a little bit more bottom end"; 160 "a little more balanced" / flatter; 4038 "big and woolly"; ribbons are "smooth" (2026 Stadium, 4:50-5:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20; 2025, 6:27-6:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg). 121 at its default (cap edge, 1 in) is "quite boomy... quite muddy" (4 Steps, 3:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y7wLssljDk).
- Dynamics: "not a huge fan" and says that is personal taste and a million great records used an SM57; the 421 is his favourite dynamic (Pt. 13 Cabs, 23:13-24:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2oSUj8oK6Pg). He finds dynamic mics add upper-mid harshness, especially at stage volume (Pt. 13 Cabs, 8:41-9:01, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2oSUj8oK6Pg). Caption "9006 Dynamic" in 2025 is probably a different model number; unconfirmed (2025, 6:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).

### Mic distance and position

- Distance: pulling back loses bass, moving in gets fuller (proximity effect); closer to cone centre gives more harsh upper end; cap edge or off-axis smooths out and warms (2025, 7:41-7:57, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg; 2026 Stadium, 5:31-6:08, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20). In 2025 he says positioning "really acts like an equalizer" (2025, 7:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
- With a ribbon he can sit more on-axis and pull the mic back to control lows (2026 Stadium, 5:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20). In 4 Steps he moves toward centre and then pulls back a few inches (4 Steps, 3:55-4:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y7wLssljDk).

### Dual cabs and the split-crossover method

- Pt. 6: his normal test tone was dual cabs: Greenback 25 with an "87 condenser" and a 160 ribbon (mic names partly garbled), "kind of like I always do" (Pt. 6 EQ, 5:20-5:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROT-mWdFlOU). Pt. 4 also used a dual cab with ribbon (Pt. 4 Sag, 26:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak).
- Pt. 13 shows a ribbon plus a 421 dynamic as a common dual-cab recipe: 160 ribbon at 6.5 in plus 421, routed with a Route A/B balance more to the dynamic (Pt. 13 Cabs, 7:09-7:42 and 19:16-19:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2oSUj8oK6Pg). His complaints: harshness from the dynamic and the extra DSP block (Pt. 13 Cabs, 8:41-9:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2oSUj8oK6Pg).
- His replacement in Pt. 13: one cab, a Split block set to Crossover after it, a Gain block on each leg, then Merge. Frequencies above the crossover go to path A and below to path B; Split A/B or Split Y will not do (Pt. 13 Cabs, 2:56-3:12 and 27:23-28:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2oSUj8oK6Pg).
  - Ribbon at 1 in instead of 6.5 in, crossover 1 kHz, +5 dB above and -2 dB below (the caption at 11:21 says "T V" for the lower figure; "plus 5 and minus 2" at 15:40 confirms it) (Pt. 13 Cabs, 10:50-11:30 and 15:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2oSUj8oK6Pg).
  - Too thin: pull the boost back to +3.5 dB above, or raise the lows by about a dB (Pt. 13 Cabs, 13:41-14:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2oSUj8oK6Pg).
  - Crossover points tried: 400, 600, 800 Hz, 1, 2, 4 kHz. 600 Hz is "slightly warmer"; 600 Hz with +4 then +3 dB above and lows up about half a dB suggested for less bite (Pt. 13 Cabs, 16:25-17:43 and 21:15-22:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2oSUj8oK6Pg).
  - 421 dynamic on its own at 1 in with the crossover: -3 dB above 1 kHz was "remarkably darker"; 2 kHz still dark; 4 kHz "warms it up a little bit and takes that edge off" and leans ribbon-like (Pt. 13 Cabs, 23:56-26:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2oSUj8oK6Pg).
  - Cuts after it: 100 Hz low cut, 10.5 kHz high cut (Pt. 13 Cabs, 5:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2oSUj8oK6Pg). He says "a standard low and high cut which is what I would set on just about any mic" but the transcript does not say whether this is the cab block's own cut or a separate EQ block; he says elsewhere he puts them after the split (27:36).
- Later he says Line 6 saw his workaround video and added a shelf EQ (Shelf, 0:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HRjwxtbiKZ0). The shelf video does not name the video, but the crossover one is the only one in this set that fits. In Pt. 35 he mentions the "split crossover technique" as one of the extras he uses (Pt. 35 Simple, 0:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F6MEgl4pBlk).
- By 2025/2026 he builds with a single cab and single mic, no dual cab.

### Low and high cut on the cab block

- Pt. 6: cuts at the cab block "aren't quite as dramatic" as the separate low/high cut block at the same frequency, which is why he uses the separate block. He says this is by ear; he did not set up a snapshot and invites viewers to try it (Pt. 6 EQ, 7:11-8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROT-mWdFlOU). 2025 repeats it: cab cuts are turned off, the EQ block gives "a more drastic slope" (2025, 21:57-22:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
- Stadium: cab-block high and low cuts turned off in favour of the EQ block (2026 Stadium, 5:01, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20). Stock factory pairings arrive with no cab cuts (Pt. 35 Simple, 4:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F6MEgl4pBlk).

## 4. IRs versus stock cabs

- He mostly uses stock cabs, which he describes as "essentially impulse responses with a few more bells and whistles" (IR, 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fxO35dEU-o). He says IRs are not bad and some are excellent (Pt. 35 Simple, 1:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F6MEgl4pBlk). He sells his own Rev 212 IR bundle (Vintage 30 speakers) on the Line 6 Marketplace and used it for the demo (IR, 3:12-3:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fxO35dEU-o).
- IR block settings: low cut, high cut, Mix (direct versus IR) and Output level. Keep Mix at 100 in most cases, since mixing in the direct signal gives a "grungy abrasive" tone. His example had no cuts, Mix 100 and level at -18 (IR, 4:55-5:14 and 8:54-9:03, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fxO35dEU-o). Level at -18 is read straight from the caption; the reason is not given.
- Presets store the IR by name, not by slot number, so reshuffling slots does not break a preset (IR, 7:49-8:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fxO35dEU-o). 128 IR slots (IR, 2:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fxO35dEU-o).
- Accepted files: Helix converts any WAV; ideal is mono, 48 kHz, 32-bit, trimmed to 2048 samples; pick the version closest to that (IR, 10:39-11:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fxO35dEU-o).
- 1024 versus 2048: lengths are samples at 48 kHz; 2048 is about 42.7 ms and 1024 about 21 ms (he reads about "0.042 seconds"; the caption then rounds) (IR, 12:19-13:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fxO35dEU-o). He cites Ben Vesco's DSP chart as 9.33 for the 1024 block and 17.33 for the 2048, with the caveat that he may be misreading the chart (IR, 14:43-15:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fxO35dEU-o). Two 1024 blocks fit on a path but only one 2048 (IR, 11:35-11:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fxO35dEU-o).
- His listening and match-EQ test: no obvious difference; a visible difference below about 75 Hz where the 2048 rolls off more steeply, and roughly 0.5 to 1 dB differences near 400 Hz and just above 1 kHz (the captions say "maybe half a dB almost down to minus 1 dB just above 1 kilohertz" and "about minus one dB around maybe 400 Hertz", both approximate); a repeat capture of the same file alone differed by about 0.1 dB (IR, 20:50-24:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fxO35dEU-o). Blind test: A was the 2048 and B the 1024; he does not say which listeners preferred (IR, 26:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fxO35dEU-o).
- Conclusion: if you want to save CPU, the 1024 block is "perfectly acceptable"; use the 2048 if you hear a difference (IR, 27:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fxO35dEU-o). Not much guitar content below 75 Hz anyway (IR, 25:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fxO35dEU-o).
- He warns about confirmation bias and about comparing different parts of the playing (IR, 24:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fxO35dEU-o).

## 5. EQ after the cab

### Which block, where, and why

- He wants the low/high cut, shelf EQ, compressor and a small reverb at the end of the chain. His reasoning for EQ and compressor last: "a more cohesive glued together sound" (Pt. 6 EQ, 12:14-12:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROT-mWdFlOU).
- EQ before or after delay and reverb is "just a personal preference thing"; tested, a "very subtle difference" unless the reverb or delay is prominent (Pt. 6 EQ, 11:49-12:14 and 46:00-47:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROT-mWdFlOU).
- Low/high cuts go before the compressor so low frequencies do not trigger it (Pt. 6 EQ, 11:06-11:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROT-mWdFlOU). In 2025 the order he builds: shelf right after amp/cab; later a parametric (cuts only) "right before the compression" (2025, 14:52 and 21:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
- Keep stereo blocks stereo downstream of stereo effects: a mono compressor after a stereo reverb collapses the stereo field (2025, 17:19-17:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).

### Shelf EQ (his "always on" EQ)

- Low and high shelf both at 650 Hz. "Not carved in stone" and "just a preference of mine" (Shelf, 3:12-3:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HRjwxtbiKZ0). He includes it in "almost all of my presets" (Shelf, 1:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HRjwxtbiKZ0). Same 650 Hz setting in 4 Steps, 2025 and 2026 Stadium (4 Steps, 6:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y7wLssljDk; 2025, 15:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg; 2026 Stadium, 13:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20).
- Below 650 Hz controls muddiness and "tubbiness"; above 650 Hz controls sizzle and "a little bit of nice fullness" (4 Steps, 7:26-7:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y7wLssljDk). Cutting below 650 "helps in that sort of 400 hertz range where things can get kind of muddy"; above 650 he likes the material around 900 Hz and the boost also balances harsher upper mids (Shelf, 8:12-8:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HRjwxtbiKZ0).
- Amounts: "one or two dB can go a long way" (4 Steps, 8:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y7wLssljDk). Examples: low -2 dB, then -3 dB; high +1 dB; "we don't need huge moves" (Shelf, 4:19-6:03, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HRjwxtbiKZ0). 2025: low -1 dB (2025, 16:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg). 2026: low -1 dB (2026 Stadium, 14:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20).
- 4 Steps starting point: highs +2 dB and lows -2 dB. But the same video later says to "start with it zeroed off", and says the cut and boost might be as small as 1 dB each (4 Steps, 7:09-7:57 and 8:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y7wLssljDk). The two statements are not reconciled, so treat +2/-2 as an illustration only.
- Rule of thumb he gives: thin and buzzy, cut the highs and boost the lows; tubby or muddy, cut the lows (4 Steps, 7:43-7:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y7wLssljDk). A way to adjust "on the fly" without touching mic position; at rehearsal or band volume it stops the tone being boomy and muddy (4 Steps, 6:37, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y7wLssljDk; Shelf, 8:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HRjwxtbiKZ0).
- Alternative splits mentioned: low at 400 and high at 1000 gives "a totally different experience"; or low shelf only below 200 (Shelf, 3:25-3:30 and 7:42-8:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HRjwxtbiKZ0).
- He says the Helix originally lacked the shelf EQ; the crossover workaround preceded it (Shelf, 0:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HRjwxtbiKZ0).

Open A string = 110 Hz, then 220, 440 (Pt. 6 EQ, 16:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROT-mWdFlOU).

- Dynamic Ambience: room size 12 m (caption "12 M"), shape "late 100" (caption), mix about 38% in 2025, about 36% in 2026; trails on (2025, 14:02-14:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg; 2026 Stadium, 11:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20). ## 8. Global EQ

- He has "never used the global EQ" (Pt. 29 GEQ, 11:03, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HXRc16e7oak).
- Manual per his reading: three parametric bands plus variable low and high cut, meant to compensate for different rooms and speakers across all setlists and presets; heard from the 1/4-inch or XLR outputs or both; never from the digital or USB outputs (caption mangles the last list; the sentence is "never from send, digital, or USB outputs") (Pt. 29 GEQ, 1:55-2:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HXRc16e7oak).
- He treats it as a corrective tool: all presets sound the same wrong way in a new room, so fix once; he cites a preset dialled at home on an FRFR at low volume sounding harsh at gig volume (Pt. 29 GEQ, 2:55-4:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HXRc16e7oak).
- Downsides of using it for tone: it does not travel with a shared or sold preset; and it is bypassed on USB, which he uses constantly for recording (Pt. 29 GEQ, 5:36-8:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HXRc16e7oak). This is why he does not put his standard low and high cuts there (Pt. 29 GEQ, 6:26-6:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HXRc16e7oak).
- Route the Global EQ to the 1/4-inch outputs only if XLR feeds front of house and 1/4-inch feeds stage monitors (Pt. 29 GEQ, 8:50-9:49, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HXRc16e7oak).
- Example fix for a muddy room: low cut around 100 Hz, about 400 Hz at Q 1.4 down "a couple dB", about 2.5 kHz same Q a gentle boost; for harsh, start high cutting (Pt. 29 GEQ, 10:05-10:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HXRc16e7oak). He stresses it depends on the situation.

## 9. How he judges a tone

- Listening gear: Sennheiser HD 600 headphones ("industry standard among mastering engineers") and says on a phone or bad earbuds you may miss low or high changes (Pt. 6 EQ, 30:36-31:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROT-mWdFlOU). 2026 Stadium: "a good set of speakers or headphones" to hear tubbiness (2026 Stadium, 12:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20).
- Level matching for A/B: always match levels between snapshots, with Channel Volume, compressor makeup or the output block, so it is not a volume contest (Pt. 4 Sag, 8:15-8:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhl9ZAQwak; 2025, 18:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
- Reference level: he gives no SPL, LUFS or dBFS target. The only level numbers are: the final Output block to balance against other presets (2025, 22:57, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg); the compressor match of about 2 dB (above); Stadium amp Level moved from -10 to 0 (2026 Stadium, 3:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20).
- Context he judges against: "mix ready" (does not fight kick, bass and cymbals), band volume and stage volume, rehearsal (Shelf, 4:00 and 8:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HRjwxtbiKZ0; 2026 Stadium, 16:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20). He often dials presets at home or in the studio and says they may need a shelf move at rehearsal (Shelf, 2:35-2:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HRjwxtbiKZ0).
- Reverb in the room: he leaves a small reverb on while building so it is not "bone dry" (4 Steps, 0:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y7wLssljDk; 2025, 13:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg). For the stock-sound test he suggests adding a subtle room verb to be fair to the dry cab (Pt. 35 Simple, 5:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F6MEgl4pBlk).
- Playing: he adjusts his touch (volume knob, soft picking, finger-picking) to what the preset gives rather than demanding it match a preconceived sound (Pt. 35 Simple, 9:42-10:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F6MEgl4pBlk).
- Pickups checked: bridge and neck (Shelf, 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HRjwxtbiKZ0; 2025, 8:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg).
- Guitars: Strat (Shelf video does not name it; 2025 is a Fender 50s Custom Shop Strat; 2026 is a 59 Les Paul Custom Shop) (2025, 4:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg; 2026 Stadium, 2:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20).

## 10. Where later advice differs from earlier

1. Dual cab (Pt. 4, 6, 13) became split-crossover on one cab (Pt. 13), then plain single cab + shelf EQ once Line 6 added the shelf EQ (Shelf, 0:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HRjwxtbiKZ0; 2025 and 2026 use a single cab).
2. Cab-block cuts versus a separate block: Pt. 6 prefers the separate block; 2025 and 2026 turn the cab cuts off and use the EQ block (Pt. 6 EQ, 7:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROT-mWdFlOU; 2025, 21:57, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgOQe3zX8Bg; 2026 Stadium, 5:01, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=488anQjzE20).
3. Low cut: 80 (Pt. 6), 100 (Pt. 13, 2025), 90 at 18 dB/oct (2026 Stadium). High cut: 10.5 kHz (Pt. 6, 13), 12 kHz (2025), 8 kHz (2026 Stadium). Treat as a range he uses: 80 to 100 Hz and 8 to 12 kHz.
4. Parametric low and high cuts: in Pt. 6 he has not tested them; in 2025 he uses the parametric block for them.
5. Pt. 35 argues that heavy processing is not required, "goes against a lot of my other videos" (Pt. 35 Simple, 25:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F6MEgl4pBlk).
6. Stadium (2026): everything in his philosophy stays; what changes is Stadium-specific (below).
